Study Tips

  1. Trace, don't memorize. The path of food, the bile pathway, and hepatic portal blood flow each tell a story. If you can narrate the journey, the names follow.
  2. Use the trace-flow quizzes. Each region has at least one. They shuffle the structures and ask you to put them back in order. Repeat until you can do it without hesitation.
  3. Memory tricks help. For abdominal muscles: hands in pockets equals external oblique direction, praying hands equals internal oblique direction.
  4. Identify the relationships. Knowing the sigmoid colon comes before the rectum is more useful than knowing either name in isolation.
  5. Lab partner quiz. Use the Round Table Method (below) to drill structures with your lab group.
  6. Cross-reference with the Digital Atlas. Names on a list become structures with shape, position, and relationships.

The Round Table Method

A 5-round group drill that takes about 15 minutes per organ system. Works best with 3 to 5 students.

Round 1: Name It

Person A points to a structure on a model or atlas. Person B names it. No notes, no peeking. Move clockwise. Anyone who misses passes the question.

Round 2: Locate It

Person A says a structure name. Person B points to it. Add a quick description of where it sits relative to a neighbor.

Round 3: Function It

Person A says a structure name. Person B states its function in one sentence. Examples: "the pyloric sphincter regulates emptying of stomach contents into the duodenum."

Round 4: Connect It

Person A says a structure name. Person B names what comes before it AND what comes after it in the relevant flow (food, bile, blood, etc.).

Round 5: Apply It

Person A invents a clinical scenario ("a patient with appendicitis"). Person B names the structure involved, where it is, and what symptoms might follow. Then trade.

Ground rules:
  • No phones during rounds.
  • Wrong answers are part of the process. Move on quickly.
  • Track which structures get missed most. Spend extra time there next round.
  • End each session by re-asking the first 3 missed items. If you can answer them, the session worked.
