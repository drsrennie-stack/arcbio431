What You Will Do

You will build a visual concept map of the GI system that pulls together everything you have learned about anatomy, digestion, absorption, and circulation. The goal is integration. By the end you should be able to point to any structure and say what it does, what comes before it, what comes after it, and what blood vessels supply or drain it.

The drawing has to carry the content. A label without a picture, or a picture without function notes, will not earn full credit.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this activity, you should be able to:

  1. Identify and label all major GI tract structures from oral cavity to anal canal, plus accessory organs.
  2. Trace the path of food, the bile pathway, and hepatic portal blood flow on the same map.
  3. Connect each region to its physiology: where does mechanical and chemical digestion happen, what gets secreted, what gets absorbed.
  4. Identify the arterial supply (celiac trunk branches, SMA, IMA) and the venous drainage (hepatic portal system).

Instructions

  1. Open the activity page using the button below to access the map template, prompts, and example.
  2. Draw the GI tract from oral cavity to anal canal. Include the esophagus, stomach (with all subdivisions), small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum), and large intestine (cecum through anal canal). Label every region.
  3. Add accessory organs: liver (with all four lobes), gallbladder, and pancreas. Show how they connect to the duodenum.
  4. Trace three pathways using different colors: path of food, bile pathway, and hepatic portal blood flow. Use arrows.
  5. Annotate physiology at each region: what digests there, what absorbs there, what gets secreted, key sphincters, key valves.
  6. Add the vasculature: celiac trunk and its three branches, SMA, IMA, and the major veins of the hepatic portal system.
  7. Submit a clear photo or scan of your completed map. Make sure all labels are legible.
