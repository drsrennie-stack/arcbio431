What You Will Do

You will build a visual concept map of the nephron that pulls together everything you have learned about renal anatomy and physiology. The goal is integration. By the end you should be able to point to any nephron segment and say what is being reabsorbed, what is being secreted, what is the driving force, and what is the clinical consequence if it fails.

The drawing has to carry the content. A label without a mechanism, or a mechanism without the structure, will not earn full credit.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this activity, you should be able to:

  1. Identify and label all nephron segments: glomerulus, Bowman capsule, PCT, loop of Henle (descending and ascending limbs), DCT, and collecting duct.
  2. Trace the path of filtrate from glomerular filtration through final urine.
  3. Trace the blood supply: afferent arteriole, glomerular capillaries, efferent arteriole, peritubular capillaries, and vasa recta.
  4. Annotate what is reabsorbed and what is secreted at each segment, including the major transporters and hormones that regulate them.
  5. Identify the structures of the juxtaglomerular apparatus and explain how they regulate GFR.

Instructions

  1. Open the activity page using the button below to access the map template, prompts, and example.
  2. Draw the nephron with all segments in the correct order: glomerulus and Bowman capsule, PCT, descending limb, ascending limb, DCT, collecting duct. Label each segment clearly.
  3. Add the blood supply: afferent arteriole, glomerular capillaries, efferent arteriole, peritubular capillaries, and vasa recta. Show the relationships clearly.
  4. Trace filtrate flow with arrows from filtration through the collecting duct to the renal pelvis. Use a separate color for blood flow.
  5. Annotate physiology at each segment: what is reabsorbed, what is secreted, the major transporters, and the hormones that regulate the process.
  6. Add the JGA: macula densa, granular cells, and extraglomerular mesangial cells. Note their function in regulating GFR and renin release.
  7. Submit a clear photo or scan of your completed map. Make sure all labels are legible.
